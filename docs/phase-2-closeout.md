## What the evidence supports

**Remote CDC and recovery.** The real isolated TCP source had no DuckPipe extension or preload. One publication covered the four fixture relations with the required replica identities, excluding source receipts. A persistent pgoutput slot served ongoing changes, with temporary per-table snapshot slots released after serial initial copy. Both fresh EXP-020 runs matched all four projections, including exact microsecond-capable timestamp normalization and both reports at held S00–S05. The runs observed S01's item 102 insert, S02's item 101 update and S03's item 102 deletion. Graceful restart recovered the offline S03 delete on the original slot. Volume-preserving consumer replacement retained exact S04 before the worker started and then reached S05. EXP-018's SIGKILL captured already-durable S03, followed by recovery to S04 and continued S05. Re-admission of previously applied work was observed, with exact current-state results. The buffered-before-durable crash window, power/host/publisher loss and wire-level exactly-once behavior remain open. [Paired keyed records](experiments/evidence/EXP-020-keyed-checkpoints.jsonl) · [EXP-018 recovery](experiments/EXP-018-cdc-recovery.md)

**Sparse retention is a mandatory blocker.** In both final paired trials an active sender and three advancing table apply positions coexisted with fixed group and slot feedback at `0/19A7DF8`. The idle customer mapping held the minimum. Three segment-switch/checkpoint epochs grew insert-to-restart span from about 0.37 MB to 40.21 MB and allocated WAL from 16,392 to 65,544 KiB, while safe headroom fell from 140,497,872 to 100,663,080 bytes. Queues drained and the fixture remained exact. EXP-018 advanced the floor with a distinct customer update and then saw another stall. Its separate loss control crossed the 128 MiB setting on the ninth forced switch/checkpoint, leaving `wal_status=lost` and a rejected reconnect despite healthy SQL. Old Bronze was stale S02 while the source held S04. A fresh catalog and slot restored S04 and continued to S05. That process discards the retired Bronze history. The cap and resnapshot bound the failure mode; sustained normal operation needs feedback progress that preserves every committed effect. [Runtime samples](experiments/evidence/EXP-020-guest-observations.jsonl) · [Loss and resnapshot](experiments/EXP-018-cdc-recovery.md#retention-crossing-reconnect-failure-and-clean-resnapshot)

**Concurrent initial COPY is a mandatory blocker.** EXP-020 guests 27, 33 and 34 each independently read committed S01 while the same order-items COPY backend remained blocked and its temporary slot was present. All three missed item 102 at the 60-second deadline and returned only 101 and 201. Each reported `STREAMING`, zero queue, no error, snapshot position `0/19AE478`, null applied position and released temporary slot. EXP-017's earlier positive control observed queued work and equal snapshot and apply positions `0/19AE438`. Both outcomes used the same image and COPY/parity code. The experiments establish a reproducible missed committed effect by the deadline. Its exact WAL record, routing outcome and persistence beyond the observation window remain unknown. Good held S00 and a successful earlier overlap are insufficient evidence for concurrent onboarding. [EXP-020 overlap runs](experiments/EXP-020-phase-2-cdc-gate.md#copy-overlap-repeats) · [Auxiliary observations](experiments/evidence/EXP-020-supplemental-observations.jsonl)

**Source-transaction visibility is a downstream constraint.** EXP-019's single named version 12 contained item 301 without order 30 and payment 302. Version 13 contained order and item without payment. Version 14 first matched S04 across all tables and reports in that run, after about 6.27 seconds. All three keys came from one committed source transaction. The deliberate split-version control and the consumer-local reader's repeated-read result are separate. S05 reached exact A-only and then exact A+B in the both-commit schedule. When B aborted, A-only remained exact and payment 303 stayed absent. Historical reads reproduced earlier exact versions while retained. The supported cutoff uses a held source, independent full parity and an explicit Bronze version. M2 requires characterizing visibility; source-atomic Bronze was never a mandatory M2 outcome. Continuous-source safe snapshots remain open under R13. [EXP-019](experiments/EXP-019-cdc-visibility.md) · [Cutoff investigation](bronze-snapshot-cutoffs.md)

**Actual storage.** The consumer image runs PostgreSQL 18.4, DuckPipe 1.0.0, pg_ducklake 0.1.0, pg_duckdb 1.1.0 and DuckDB 1.5.1. The PostgreSQL metadata schema is `ducklake`, catalog format **0.4**, with a local root `/var/lib/postgresql/18/docker/pg_ducklake` on the consumer named volume. Typical Phase 2 writes produced four inline table records and zero referenced data or delete files. The quiescent order-items flush in both EXP-020 runs produced one 2,208-byte Parquet file with 11 data records and one 1,229-byte Parquet file with 8 delete positions. The bounded adapter checked resolved paths, sizes, header/footer magic and digests. Logical S05 and retained S04 still matched after materialization. EXP-015 independently held the worker stopped while replacing the container and verified identical catalog and physical-file persistence. A catalog version can be readable with inline data alone. Phase 3 must prove actual S3 access to the file-backed part of the same writer catalog.
